\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
Kimodo-Policy adapts a pretrained motion generator into a visually grounded humanoid policy through root--body--hand generation and constrained motion-history completion. The experiments support transferring motion structure to closed-loop loco-manipulation, with additional robot policy pretraining improving multi-task and real-world results but not every task setting. Our evaluation is restricted to the SONIC control stack: kinematic predictions alone do not ensure dynamic feasibility, and transfer across trackers is unverified. Small physical-task sample sizes, mixed literature comparisons, and incomplete isolation of architectural components limit stronger conclusions. Future work can examine broader motion priors, tracker-independent interfaces, and complementary vision--language or video conditioning.
