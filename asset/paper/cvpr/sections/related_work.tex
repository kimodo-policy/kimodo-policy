\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}
\paragraph{Humanoid action models.}
VLM-based humanoid policies transfer visual-semantic representations to action prediction~\cite{grootn1,psi0,humanoidexo,openhlm}; video-based policies instead exploit visual dynamics~\cite{motionwam,wholebodywam_wbc}. These foundations interact with different action interfaces: unified predictors share a generator across components~\cite{openhlm,motionwam}, hybrid interfaces couple upper-body targets to locomotion commands~\cite{humanoidexo,wholebodyvla}, and HAF progressively expands an action set through hierarchical generation~\cite{haf}. Kimodo-Policy directly adapts an existing motion generator and preserves its root-to-body factorization, adding a specialized hand stage. Its contribution is this particular transfer and control mechanism, rather than the general idea of using motion knowledge in robotics.

\paragraph{Motion generation and transfer.}
Text-to-motion methods range from variational models~\cite{temos} to diffusion in pose or latent space~\cite{mdm,mld} and discrete-token generation~\cite{t2mgpt,momask}. OmniControl introduces flexible spatial constraints~\cite{omnicontrol}; Kimodo scales controllable generation with a root--body hierarchy and humanoid-compatible representations~\cite{kimodo}. Motion learning already supports humanoid foundation models: Humanoid-VLA and ZeroWBC incorporate motion priors within language or vision--language models~\cite{humanoidvla,zerowbc}, MotionWAM adapts a video model through motion training~\cite{motionwam}, and WholeBodyWAM integrates a pretrained motion expert with video and action experts~\cite{wholebodywam_motionpriors}. We study direct adaptation of a frozen diffusion-based motion generator, with visual conditioning and measured history supporting repeated action-chunk prediction.
