\begin{abstract}
Humanoid loco-manipulation requires coordinating locomotion, posture, and interaction from visual observations. We investigate Kinematic Action Models (KAMs), which use a pretrained motion generator as the action-generating backbone and adapt its movement structure to closed-loop control. Our instantiation, Kimodo-Policy, grounds frozen Kimodo root and body denoisers in egocentric observations through trainable ControlNet branches. A dedicated hand head completes a root--body--hand hierarchy, while constrained motion-history completion connects successive action chunks. Policy pretraining on 419 hours of humanoid demonstrations learns visual conditioning and hand control without updating the motion backbone. With 678M total and 92M trainable parameters, the policy reaches 72.14\% success on multi-task HumanoidArena, 75.83\% across eight SIMPLE tasks, and 60\% across eight physical-robot tasks. Removing the pretrained motion backbone reduces success from 51.67\% to 18.33\% in a three-task ablation. These results support motion generation as a reusable foundation for visually grounded humanoid control, while revealing task-dependent effects of the motion prior and additional policy pretraining.
\end{abstract}
