A BSTRACT Humanoid vision–language–action (VLA) models translate multimodal task un- derstanding into coordinated whole-body behavior. Existing approaches equip pretrained vision–language or video generation backbones with action experts, but their pretraining does not directly establish whole-body motion priors. Uni- fied or upper–lower-body action designs also lack dedicated root, body, and hand modeling despite their distinct requirements. These limitations increase the bur- den of learning precise, coordinated actions from limited task-specific humanoid demonstrations. We introduce Kimodo-Policy, which initializes visually grounded control with pretrained text-to-motion (T2M) priors over inter-joint coordination and temporal structure. Its three-stage root–body–hand architecture combines dedicated generators with explicit dependencies: root trajectories condition body generation, while future body features and visual observations guide hand con- trol. We pretrain the policy on diverse humanoid demonstrations, conditioning actions on visual observations, language instructions, and motion history. With 678M total and only 92M trainable parameters, Kimodo-Policy achieves strong performance on HumanoidArena, SIMPLE, and real-world loco-manipulation tasks. Learning instruction-conditioned behaviors from demonstrations provides a unified mapping from multimodal inputs to robot actions. Extending VLA learning to humanoids could broaden task coverage in human environments, where locomo- tion, posture, and manipulation must be coordinated (Bjorck et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2026a; Hu et al., 2026). For example, transferring objects between tables requires grasping, turning, walking, and placement while maintaining balance (Fig. 1, bottom). Such tasks require grounding task in- tent and visual feedback in coherent whole-body actions, motivating the extension of robot policy architectures to humanoid loco-manipulation. Humanoid policy learning commonly extends the backbone–action expert paradigm from manipula- tion to whole-body control. Pretrained vision–language models encode instruction and observation semantics (Fig. 2(a)) (Bjorck et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2026a; Hu et al., 2026), while video gen- erators supply spatiotemporal priors for scene evolution (Fig. 2(b)) (Zheng et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026). These representations condition action generation across root, body, and hand motion. Uni- fied predictors generate whole-body sequences (Fig. 2(d)) (Hu et al., 2026; Zheng et al., 2026); hybrid designs combine upper-body joint targets with lower-body locomotion commands executed by dedicated controllers (Fig. 2(e)) (Zhong et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025). The former captures cross-component dependencies; the latter couples upper-body control with learned locomotion and stabilization. Both transfer pretrained semantic and visual dynamics knowledge to humanoid loco- manipulation. Despite these advances, two challenges remain. ❶ Insufficient Whole-Body Motion Priors. Com- pared with fixed-base manipulation, humanoid control requires higher-dimensional inter-joint and temporal coordination. Backbone pretraining (Bjorck et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2026a; Zheng et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026) does not directly equip action generators with priors aligned with target hu- manoid kinematics, leaving them to learn these priors, often from limited task-specific demonstra- tions. ❷ Insufficient Differentiation of Motion Components. Root, body, and hand control differ in scale and precision. Unified prediction (Hu et al., 2026; Zheng et al., 2026) may create competing objectives; hybrid execution (Zhong et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025) does not itself provide dedicated root, body, and hand generators. Both must accommodate component-specific requirements while maintaining whole-body coordination. Recent text-to-motion (T2M) methods learn structured motion distributions conditioned on text or spatial constraints (Petrovich et al., 2022; Tevet et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023a; Guo et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2024). VLM-based methods transfer visual-semantic knowledge to actions (Bjorck et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2026a; Zhong et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2026): GR00T N1 (Bjorck et al., 2025) couples a VLM with a diffusion action model, while Ψ 0 (Wei et al., 2026a) transfers representa- tions from human egocentric demonstrations to a humanoid action expert. VGM-based methods exploit visual dynamics priors (Zheng et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026): MotionWAM (Zheng et al., 2026) generates whole-body motion from intermediate video features; WholeBodyWAM (Li et al., 2026) grounds world–action priors in whole-body controllers. Unified generators (Hu et al., 2026; Zheng et al., 2026) share prediction across motion components; hybrids (Zhong et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025) combine upper-body targets with locomotion commands. HAF (Gu et al., 2026) stages generation with a shared action expert, expanding the active action set and refining earlier predic- tions. Kimodo-Policy instead transfers a T2M backbone with pretrained inter-joint and temporal priors, reducing the need to learn motion regularities from humanoid demonstrations. Dedicated root, body, and hand generators meet distinct control requirements, while cross-stage conditioning supports coordinated, visually grounded whole-body behavior. 2.2 T EXT - TO -M OTION (T2M) T2M includes TEMOS’s variational language–motion modeling (Petrovich et al., 2022), pose-space MDM (Tevet et al., 2023) and latent-space MLD (Chen et al., 2023) diffusion, and autoregressive T2M-GPT (Zhang et al., 2023a) and masked MoMask (Guo et al., 2024) quantized token generation. OmniControl (Xie et al., 2024) adds spatial constraints; Kimodo (Rempe et al., 2026) trains con- trollable root–body diffusion hierarchies at scale for human and humanoid skeletons. Humanoid- VLA (Ding et al., 2025) and ZeroWBC (Yang et al., 2026) learn autoregressive motion priors in pretrained language models and VLMs before visual integration and task adaptation, respectively. MotionWAM (Zheng et al., 2026) adapts a pretrained video world model via staged video–motion training; WholeBodyWAM (Zhang et al., 2026) integrates a pretrained motion expert with video and action experts. Kimodo-Policy directly reuses a diffusion T2M backbone pretrained at scale in robot kinematic space. Dedicated root–body–hand generators ground inter-joint and temporal pri- ors in vision and motion history for closed-loop loco-manipulation, reducing relearning from robot demonstrations while meeting distinct control requirements. 3
