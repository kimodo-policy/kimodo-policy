Learning instruction-conditioned behaviors from demonstrations provides a unified mapping from multimodal inputs to robot actions. Extending VLA learning to humanoids could broaden task coverage in human environments, where locomo- tion, posture, and manipulation must be coordinated (Bjorck et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2026a; Hu et al., 2026). For example, transferring objects between tables requires grasping, turning, walking, and placement while maintaining balance (Fig. 1, bottom). Such tasks require grounding task in- tent and visual feedback in coherent whole-body actions, motivating the extension of robot policy architectures to humanoid loco-manipulation. Humanoid policy learning commonly extends the backbone–action expert paradigm from manipula- tion to whole-body control. Pretrained vision–language models encode instruction and observation semantics (Fig. 2(a)) (Bjorck et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2026a; Hu et al., 2026), while video gen- erators supply spatiotemporal priors for scene evolution (Fig. 2(b)) (Zheng et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026). These representations condition action generation across root, body, and hand motion. Uni- fied predictors generate whole-body sequences (Fig. 2(d)) (Hu et al., 2026; Zheng et al., 2026); hybrid designs combine upper-body joint targets with lower-body locomotion commands executed by dedicated controllers (Fig. 2(e)) (Zhong et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025). The former captures cross-component dependencies; the latter couples upper-body control with learned locomotion and stabilization. Both transfer pretrained semantic and visual dynamics knowledge to humanoid loco- manipulation. Despite these advances, two challenges remain. ❶ Insufficient Whole-Body Motion Priors. Com- pared with fixed-base manipulation, humanoid control requires higher-dimensional inter-joint and temporal coordination. Backbone pretraining (Bjorck et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2026a; Zheng et al., 2026; Li et al., 2026) does not directly equip action generators with priors aligned with target hu- manoid kinematics, leaving them to learn these priors, often from limited task-specific demonstra- tions. ❷ Insufficient Differentiation of Motion Components. Root, body, and hand control differ in scale and precision. Unified prediction (Hu et al., 2026; Zheng et al., 2026) may create competing objectives; hybrid execution (Zhong et al., 2025; Jiang et al., 2025) does not itself provide dedicated root, body, and hand generators.
