This section presents Kimodo-Policy, an instantiation of KAMs for closed-loop humanoid loco-manipulation. We first introduce the pretrained motion backbone and visual conditioning mechanism (Sec.~\ref{sec:method_preliminaries}), then describe the root--body--hand architecture (Sec.~\ref{sec:method_architecture}). We next explain how motion-history conditioning adapts motion generation to closed-loop interaction (Sec.~\ref{sec:t2m_transfer}), and conclude with the training objective and inference procedure (Sec.~\ref{sec:training_inference}).

\subsection{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:method_preliminaries}

\textbf{Kimodo as a KAM Backbone.} Kimodo-Policy instantiates Kinematic Action Models (KAMs) by using a pretrained motion generator as the backbone for humanoid action generation. We adopt Kimodo-G1~\citep{kimodo}, which generates robot kinematic motion from language instructions and optional motion constraints through a hierarchical root--body denoising process. The root stage models global translation and heading, while the body stage generates articulated motion conditioned on the root prediction. Its motion representation jointly describes root motion, joint positions, velocities, orientations, and foot contacts, providing a structured space for whole-body prediction. Pretraining in this space allows the backbone to acquire temporal and cross-body coordination patterns that can be reused during downstream policy learning. We retain this pretrained generation structure and keep the backbone frozen, learning additional conditioning modules to adapt its predictions to robot interaction. Motion generation thus provides the foundation of the policy, while adapting it to closed-loop control requires grounding its predictions in the current scene and execution state.

\textbf{ControlNet for Visual Conditioning.} To make the pretrained motion generator responsive to the current scene, we adopt ControlNet-based conditioning~\citep{zhang2023adding} for the frozen root and body denoisers. Trainable control branches process egocentric visual features and inject residual signals into selected layers of the corresponding denoisers. The injection projections are initialized to zero, so the root--body generator initially follows its pretrained computation before learning to incorporate visual information. This design separates the pretrained motion backbone from the parameters learned for visual conditioning, allowing robot demonstrations to shape scene-dependent motion predictions without updating the backbone weights. Within this KAM instantiation, the motion generator supplies learned whole-body structure, while ControlNet provides a trainable interface through which observations guide action generation.

\subsection{Model Architecture}
\label{sec:method_architecture}

\textbf{Problem Formulation.} We formulate language-conditioned humanoid loco-manipulation as a closed-loop prediction problem in which the policy generates short, structured action chunks that combine root motion, articulated body motion, and hand controls. At environment step $k$, the policy receives a natural-language instruction $\ell$, the current egocentric RGB image $I_k$, an available motion history $\mathcal{H}_k=[\mathbf{x}_{k-L+1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_k]$ encoded in the Kimodo representation, and the current bilateral hand state $\mathbf{s}^{h}_k$. It predicts a future chunk of $K$ frames, with each action $\mathbf{a}_i=[\mathbf{r}_i,\mathbf{b}_i,\mathbf{h}_i]$ comprising root motion, articulated body motion, and left/right hand-control signals:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{A}_{k+1:k+K}\sim\pi_{\theta}(\,\cdot\mid I_k,\ell,\mathcal{H}_k,\mathbf{s}^{h}_k).
  \label{eq:policy_formulation}
\end{equation}
The root and body components provide kinematic references for a low-level motion tracker~\citep{sonic}, while the hand component specifies synchronized closure commands with binary or continuous parameterizations according to the task interface. The policy executes a chunk prefix and replans from updated observations, using visual feedback and motion history to guide subsequent predictions.

\textbf{Three-Stage Architecture.}
As illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:method_architecture}, Kimodo-Policy extends Kimodo's~\citep{kimodo} pretrained motion generator into a three-stage root--body--hand architecture.
LLM2Vec~\citep{behnamghader2024llm2veclargelanguagemodels} encodes the instruction, while frozen DINOv3~\citep{dinov3} extracts egocentric visual features.
Root and body ControlNet branches incorporate these visual features through Dual-Path Fusion and inject the resulting residuals into the corresponding frozen denoisers.
The inter-stage dependencies are summarized as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}^{r}_k = \mathcal{G}_{r}(\mathbf{c}_k),
\quad
\mathbf{z}^{b}_k = \mathcal{G}_{b}\!\left(\mathbf{c}_k,\operatorname{sg}(\mathbf{z}^{r}_k)\right),
\quad
\widehat{\mathbf{U}}_k = \mathcal{G}_{h}\!\left(\mathbf{s}^{h}_k,\operatorname{sg}(\mathbf{V}_k),\operatorname{sg}(\mathbf{z}^{b}_k)\right),
\label{eq:three_stage_architecture}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{c}_k$ collects the instruction embedding, motion history, and visual features $\mathbf{V}_k$;
$\mathbf{z}^{r}_k$ and $\mathbf{z}^{b}_k$ collectively denote each stage's motion predictions and conditioning features;
and $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ denotes stop-gradient.
Sampling noise is omitted for brevity.
The root stage predicts global translation and heading, whose local representation conditions the body stage to generate articulated motion.
The trainable HandNet then combines future body features, visual features, and the current hand state to predict bilateral hand controls $\widehat{\mathbf{U}}_k$.
All root-to-body conditioning is detached, preventing body-stage gradients from reaching either Kimodo-Root or ControlNet-Root.
Likewise, body and visual features supplied to HandNet are detached, preventing hand supervision from updating Kimodo-Body or the ControlNet branches.
These gradient boundaries separate optimization across the three stages while preserving their forward dependencies for coordinated generation.
Finally, root and body predictions are converted into whole-body tracking references and executed alongside the hand commands.

\textbf{Dual-Path Fusion.} To ground the frozen motion prior in the current scene, we encode the egocentric image into spatial tokens with DINOv3~\citep{dinov3} and process them through trainable root and body ControlNet branches. At selected layers of each Kimodo denoiser, we fuse visual context through two complementary paths. In the cross-attention path, future-motion hidden states serve as queries over the image tokens, allowing each predicted time step to retrieve task-relevant visual evidence. In parallel, a token-axis MLP maps the spatial image-token sequence to the future temporal horizon, providing a direct image-to-time correspondence. We sum the two outputs and pass them through a layer-specific zero-initialized projection to form a residual hint for the corresponding Kimodo layer. These hints are added only to future-motion tokens, leaving the constrained history prefix unchanged. The two paths jointly support content-selective visual retrieval and temporally aligned conditioning.

\textbf{HandNet.} Hand control has different output semantics from root and body motion: it specifies when each hand should open or close, rather than a kinematic pose trajectory. We therefore model bilateral hand control with a dedicated diffusion head. At each denoising step, HandNet jointly processes the current hand state, noisy future hand controls, future body hidden states, and visual tokens, and predicts two hand-control values for every future frame. Conditioning on the predicted body and current scene aligns interaction commands with the evolving whole-body action, while a separate head avoids placing hand labels in the root--body motion space. The head supports binary open/close states and continuous closure changes. During training, its body and visual conditioning features are detached, so hand supervision updates HandNet without sending gradients into the motion denoiser or ControlNet.

\subsection{Adapting Motion Priors for Closed-Loop Control}
\label{sec:t2m_transfer}

\textbf{Grounding Language-Aligned Motion Priors.} Kimodo's motion pretraining associates language semantics with coordinated root and body movements~\citep{kimodo}, as illustrated by the sequence generated by the original model in the upper row of Figure~\ref{fig:t2m_transfer}. This language-aligned motion prior provides the generative foundation for Kimodo-Policy. Real-world interaction additionally requires the generated motion to account for the observed scene and the robot's ongoing execution. We learn visual conditioning from robot demonstrations to adapt the inherited motion-generation process to the current scene, while motion-history constraints anchor each prediction to the robot's executed trajectory. The lower row illustrates the resulting closed-loop policy in real-world object transfer. Within this KAM instantiation, policy learning therefore centers on grounding an existing language-aligned motion prior in the environment and execution context, with the pretrained generator retained as the primary action backbone.

\textbf{Motion-History Conditioning.} Applying Kimodo's pretrained sequence generator to closed-loop control requires reconciling coherent motion synthesis with repeated, observation-dependent action selection: the policy must predict a finite action chunk, execute only part of it, and revise subsequent predictions using updated observations. Replaying a sequence generated once cannot account for deviations between predicted and measured motion or changes in the visual scene. We address this mismatch by formulating each policy query as constrained motion completion in Kimodo's kinematic representation. At every replanning step, the observed motion history is placed in the prefix of a history--future window, and a feature-wise mask specifies which kinematic values are available. The observed prefix is restored before each denoising step, while the unconstrained future is generated as the next action chunk. In parallel, trainable ControlNet branches inject features from the current egocentric image into Kimodo's root and body denoisers, grounding the generated motion in the scene while retaining the pretrained root--body structure. The policy executes a prefix of the predicted chunk through the motion tracker; the resulting state history and updated image then condition the next prediction. This formulation turns Kimodo's constraint-conditioned sequence generator into a receding-horizon controller, preserving its structured motion prior while enabling continual visual and state-based correction.

\subsection{Training and Inference}
\label{sec:training_inference}

\textbf{Training Objective.} We train the ControlNet branches and HandNet with supervised denoising on demonstration windows. For each window, we sample a diffusion timestep and perturb the normalized root--body features and hand targets with Gaussian noise. A feature-wise mask imposes the observed history as a constraint, while supervision is applied to valid future frames. The objective is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}=\lambda_r\mathcal{L}_{r}+\lambda_b\mathcal{L}_{b}
  +\lambda_h\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{h}+\lambda_{\Delta}\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}\bigr),
  \label{eq:policy_training_objective}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{r}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{b}$ are masked mean squared errors between the predicted clean root and body features, respectively, and their normalized targets, evaluated over valid future frames. For binary control, hand states are encoded in $[-1,1]$; for continuous control, HandNet predicts closure residuals relative to the latest valid observed hand state. The hand reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L}_{h}$ is a masked mean squared error on these targets. The transition loss $\mathcal{L}_{\Delta}$ penalizes the $\ell_1$ error between predicted and demonstrated consecutive hand-control changes, including the transition from the observed state to the first future frame. In continuous mode, predicted residuals are converted back to closure values before computing this loss. Motion losses exclude padded frames, while hand losses additionally mask unavailable labels; transition terms are evaluated only when both adjacent labels are valid. For the main MSE configuration, we use $(\lambda_r,\lambda_b,\lambda_h,\lambda_{\Delta})=(4,1,1,0.2)$. We detach the predicted root before body generation and, in the main configuration, detach root ControlNet features passed to the body branch. HandNet also receives detached body and visual features. These detachments regulate gradient flow between trainable branches, while the Kimodo denoisers and DINOv3~\citep{dinov3} encoder remain frozen.

\textbf{Inference.} At each policy query, we encode the language instruction and current egocentric image, and represent the available proprioceptive history in Kimodo's motion space with frame- and feature-wise observation masks. We initialize the future motion and hand sequences from Gaussian noise and iteratively denoise them with DDIM~\citep{song2022denoisingdiffusionimplicitmodels}. At each step, the observed history is restored, visual features are injected into the frozen root and body denoisers through ControlNet, the predicted root conditions body generation, and HandNet predicts hand controls from the current hand state, visual features, and future body features. After the final denoising step, we invert the motion representation and map the resulting root and joint trajectories, together with bilateral hand commands, to tracker-compatible references. The low-level whole-body tracker executes a prefix of the predicted chunk; the policy then incorporates the updated visual observation and proprioceptive state to replan. This receding-horizon loop combines structured motion generation with continual closed-loop correction.
